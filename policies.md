# CryoPAF Galactic Bulge Pulsar Survey (GBps) — Collaboration Policies & Guidelines

This document outlines the policies and guidelines for all members of the cryoPAF Galactic Bulge Pulsar Survey (GBps) collaboration. These guidelines are designed to promote open communication, prevent duplicate efforts, and ensure fair credit across our team.

---

## 1. Data Access and Project Proposals

**Data Access:** All members of the collaboration are granted full access to the survey data products.

**Project Initiation:** Any member wishing to use survey data for a specific science project, including work intended for publication, conference presentations or posters, must first submit a project proposal to the collaboration.

**Proposal Submission Requirements:** Proposers must circulate a brief project description (outlining scientific goals, methodology, and required data) along with the list of lead investigators to the entire collaboration.

**Review and Approval:** The Principal Investigator (PI: Shi Dai) is tasked with evaluating submitted project proposals. Collaboration members will have a designated window to review the proposal and offer feedback or express concerns regarding overlap with existing projects. If concerns arise, the PI will work directly with the proposers to refine the scope and resolve conflicts before the project proceeds. Once approved by the PI, approved projects will be listed on the survey webpage.

## 2. Conference Presentations and Posters

**Advance Notification:** Members planning to present survey results, discoveries, or follow-up studies at any conference, workshop, or seminar must notify the PI and the wider collaboration well in advance of the abstract submission deadline.

**Team Coordination:** When multiple collaboration members plan to attend the same meeting, they are expected to communicate and coordinate their topics and presentation materials. This ensures broad, cohesive representation of the survey and avoids redundant talks or posters.

**Acknowledgments:** All presentations, slides, and posters using survey data must prominently acknowledge the survey collaboration and explicitly recognise the contributions of other team members (such as those involved in observations, pipeline development).

## 3. Survey-Related Follow-Up Proposals

**Telescope and Grant Proposals:** Members planning to submit observing proposals (e.g., for pulsar timing, multi-wavelength follow-up studies) or funding proposals targeting pulsars discovered by the survey must inform the collaboration well in advance of submission.

**Collaborative Input:** Proposers should share their plans with the team early enough to solicit constructive suggestions, technical comments, and potential co-investigators, maximising the scientific return and success rate for the collaboration as a whole.

## 4. Paper Authorship and Publications

**Project Approval Prerequisite:** Publications utilising survey data are expected to originate from formally proposed and approved collaboration projects.

**Internal Review:** Lead authors must circulate a complete draft of the manuscript to the entire collaboration well ahead of the intended journal submission date (e.g., at least two to three weeks prior) to allow sufficient time for internal review, comments, and feedback.

**Opt-In Authorship Model:** Collaboration members are not automatically included on every survey paper. Lead authors must issue an explicit invitation to the collaboration for co-authorship, and only those who actively respond to the invitation will be listed on the author list.

**Authorship Criteria:** To ensure fair credit across our team, co-authorship is reserved for members who have made significant contributions to the specific project or the survey that enabled it (e.g., survey design, regular meeting/discussion, observing, data processing, instrumentation, analysis, or substantial manuscript editing). Members should only sign up for authorship when they meet this standard.
